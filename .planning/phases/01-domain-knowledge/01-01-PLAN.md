  <action>
Create a comprehensive FNOL (First Notice of Loss) lifecycle reference document covering:

1. **FNOL Data Capture Checklist** — Every data field captured at intake:
   - Policy number, insured name, contact info
   - Date/time/location of loss
   - Incident description (narrative)
   - Other party information (name, insurance, contact)
   - Police report number (if applicable)
   - Witness information
   - Photo metadata/references
   - Injury status (any injuries reported → expedite flag)
   - Vehicle information (year, make, model, VIN, mileage)

2. **Claim Categorization** — The 6 auto claim categories with when each applies:
   - Collision: vehicle hits another vehicle or object
   - Comprehensive: non-collision damage (theft, vandalism, weather, animal strike, glass)
   - Liability: insured is at-fault, other party files against insured's policy
   - Theft: vehicle stolen (full theft) or parts stolen (partial)
   - Vandalism: intentional damage by third party
   - Weather/CAT: hail, flood, tornado, hurricane — comprehensive coverage, may trigger CAT event tag

   Explain WHY categorization at intake matters: determines which coverage type applies, routes to correct adjuster path, affects deductible amount, impacts fraud analysis patterns.

3. **FNOL-to-Payment Lifecycle Stages** — Complete sequential flow:
   - Stage 1: FNOL Receipt (Front Desk) — data capture, categorization, claim ID generation, acknowledgment timestamp
   - Stage 2: Coverage Verification (Claims Officer) — policy lookup, active status, coverage type match, exclusion check, deductible lookup
   - Stage 3: Damage Assessment (Assessor) — estimate generation, total loss check, OEM/aftermarket, rental days
   - Stage 4: Fraud Analysis (Fraud Analyst) — pattern matching, scoring, SIU referral recommendation
   - Stage 5: Senior Review (Senior Reviewer) — approval/denial/escalation decision, FCSP timeline check
   - Stage 6: Payment (Finance) — calculation, subrogation flag, payment record

   For each stage: what data enters, what data exits, why this stage must come before the next.

4. **CAT Event Tagging** — When and why to tag a claim as catastrophic:
   - Multiple claims from same geographic area in same time window
   - Date/location matches known weather event
   - Affects processing priority and coverage type (weather = comprehensive)

5. **Urgency Indicators** — What triggers expedited processing:
   - Injury reported
   - Total loss likely
   - Rental vehicle needed immediately
   - Legal representation letter received

Format as a reference document with clear headers, suitable for embedding into AGENTS.md. Use principles and reasoning frameworks, NOT hardcoded rules. Include a "Quick Reference" section at the top with the most critical facts.

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: Create coverage verification knowledge base</name>
  <files>reference/domain-knowledge/coverage-verification.md</files>
  <action>
Create a comprehensive coverage verification reference document covering:

1. **Policy Lookup Protocol** — How coverage verification works:
   - Look up policy by policy number
   - Verify policy is active (check effective dates, not lapsed)
   - Lapsed policy grace period: most states allow 10-30 day grace period — if loss occurred within grace period, coverage may still apply
   - Match claim category to coverage types on policy
   - Identify applicable coverage limits

2. **Coverage Types on Auto Policies** — What each covers:
   - Liability (bodily injury + property damage to others)
   - Collision (damage to insured vehicle from collision)
   - Comprehensive (damage from non-collision events)
   - Uninsured Motorist (UM) — at-fault party has no insurance
   - Underinsured Motorist (UIM) — at-fault party has insufficient insurance
   - Medical Payments / Personal Injury Protection (PIP)
   - Rental Reimbursement
   - Roadside Assistance/Towing

3. **Exclusion Types** — Common auto policy exclusions with reasoning:
   - Intentional acts (policyholder deliberately caused damage)
   - Racing/competition use
   - Commercial use on personal policy (e.g., Uber/Lyft on personal auto)
   - Excluded drivers (specifically named on policy as excluded)
   - Non-owned vehicle without endorsement
   - Mechanical breakdown (not an insured peril)
   - Wear and tear / maintenance failures
   - Nuclear/war/government action (standard exclusions)

   For each: what triggers the exclusion check, what evidence to look for, what the denial path looks like.

4. **Deductible Determination** — How deductibles work:
   - Per-coverage deductibles (collision typically $500, comprehensive typically $250)
   - Higher deductible = lower premium trade-off
   - Named-driver deductibles may vary
   - Deductible waiver scenarios (e.g., not-at-fault collision in some states)
   - Deductible applies to damage payout, not to liability

5. **UM/UIM Identification** — When to route to uninsured/underinsured path:
   - At-fault party has no insurance → UM coverage on insured's policy
   - At-fault party has insurance but insufficient limits → UIM coverage
   - Different deductibles and limits than collision
   - Requires identification of at-fault party's insurance status early

6. **Coverage Determination Outcomes** — The four possible results:
   - COVERED: Active policy, matching coverage, no exclusions → proceed to assessment
   - DENIED: Exclusion applies, lapsed policy (past grace), no matching coverage → Senior Reviewer for denial documentation
   - CONDITIONAL: Coverage uncertain, needs additional investigation → hold for review
   - UM/UIM_ROUTE: At-fault party uninsured/underinsured → different coverage path

Format as reasoning frameworks. Include a mock policy database description (5 records: standard active, lapsed, excluded driver, high deductible, comprehensive-only) to illustrate verification scenarios.
