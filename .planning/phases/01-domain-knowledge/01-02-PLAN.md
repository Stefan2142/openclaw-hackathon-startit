Create a comprehensive damage assessment reference document covering:

1. **Damage Estimation Methodology** — How auto damage estimates work:
   - Labor cost estimation: labor hours x labor rate (body, mechanical, paint rates vary)
   - Parts cost: replacement parts pricing
   - Paint and materials
   - Supplemental costs (towing, storage if applicable)
   - The estimate is a professional judgment, not a formula — explain what factors an experienced adjuster considers
   - Photo-based vs in-person inspection — for the hackathon, the Assessor agent accepts structured damage descriptions and photo references as input (not live ML inference)

2. **Ohio Total Loss Determination** — The critical state-specific rule:
   - Ohio uses the 100% ACV (Actual Cash Value) rule
   - If repair cost equals or exceeds the vehicle's ACV → total loss
   - Most states use 70-80% thresholds; Ohio is stricter (100%)
   - ACV = fair market value of vehicle immediately before the loss, accounting for age, mileage, condition, local market
   - When total loss is declared: insurer pays ACV minus deductible, takes salvage
   - Salvage value estimation principles
   - Policyholder's right to retain salvage (reduced payout)

3. **OEM vs Aftermarket Parts Decision Framework**:
   - OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) parts: exact fit, maintain vehicle value, higher cost
   - Aftermarket parts: may fit well, lower cost, can affect vehicle value and safety
   - Framework principle: vehicles under 3 years old or under 36,000 miles → recommend OEM parts
   - Older/higher mileage → aftermarket acceptable with policyholder disclosure
   - Some states mandate OEM for newer vehicles — frame as judgment principle, not hardcoded rule
   - Impact on diminished value claims

4. **Rental Reimbursement Calculation**:
   - Standard rental allowance: typically $30-50/day (varies by policy)
   - Rental days tied to estimated repair timeline
   - Total loss: typically capped at 10 days (time to locate replacement)
   - Rental coverage must be on the policy (check with Claims Officer output)

5. **Pre-existing Damage Detection**:
   - Indicators: rust on fresh impact areas, paint oxidation on damaged panels, damage in non-impact zones
   - Damage inconsistent with described incident mechanics
   - Prior repair evidence (Bondo, mismatched paint, non-OEM parts already installed)
   - Pre-existing damage reduces estimate — only incident-related damage is covered
   - Flag for conditional approval pending physical inspection

6. **Hidden Damage Likelihood**:
   - Structural damage behind panels (not visible in initial assessment)
   - Frame damage indicators (uneven gaps, door alignment issues)
   - Suspension/alignment issues from impact
   - Flag as "supplement likely" in estimate — Senior Reviewer may pre-authorize supplement threshold

Format as reasoning frameworks. Include a "Quick Reference" section with Ohio-specific numbers the team must know cold.

Create a comprehensive fraud detection pattern catalog covering:

1. **Soft Fraud vs Hard Fraud Distinction** — Critical framework:
   - Hard fraud: deliberately fabricated claims (staged accident, phantom claim, arson)
     - Response: investigate → refer to SIU → likely deny → potential criminal prosecution
   - Soft fraud: exaggeration of legitimate claims (inflated repair estimate, claiming pre-existing damage as new, adding injuries that didn't happen)
     - Response: investigate → negotiate reduced settlement → flag for future claims
   - The distinction matters legally: hard fraud is criminal, soft fraud is civil
   - Fraud Analyst must classify which type and recommend accordingly

2. **Named Fraud Patterns** — Minimum 7 patterns with detailed descriptions:

   **Hard Fraud Patterns:**
   - **Staged Accident / Swoop-and-Squat**: Fraudster cuts in front of victim and brakes suddenly. Often involves multiple vehicles and coordinated participants. Indicators: low-speed rear-end, multiple occupants claiming injury, witnesses who are friends of claimant.
   - **Phantom Passengers / Jump-In Claims**: People not in the vehicle at time of accident claim they were passengers and file injury claims. Indicators: passenger count exceeds vehicle capacity, no mention of passengers in police report, passengers file claims days later.
   - **Paper Accident**: Accident never happened. Fraudsters fabricate all documentation — fake police reports, fake repair estimates, fake medical records. Indicators: no independent witnesses, no physical evidence, repair shop has prior fraud associations, claimant has history of similar claims.

   **Soft Fraud Patterns:**
   - **Inflated Repair Estimates**: Real accident, but repair shop and/or claimant inflate damage. Indicators: estimate significantly above comparable claims, labor hours excessive for damage type, parts pricing above market, damage doesn't match described incident.
   - **Prior Damage / VIN Switching**: Claiming pre-existing damage as new. Indicators: rust/oxidation on "fresh" damage, damage inconsistent with incident mechanics, VIN plate tampering, vehicle history shows prior similar damage.

   **Either Category:**
   - **Owner Give-Up (vehicle abandonment)**: Owner stages theft or total loss of vehicle they want to get rid of (underwater on loan, mechanical problems). Indicators: vehicle found stripped, owner recently had major mechanical diagnosis, loan balance exceeds ACV.
   - **Organized Fraud Ring**: Multiple related claimants filing against the same event or pattern. Indicators: shared addresses, shared phone numbers, same repair shop, same attorney, claims filed in clusters.

3. **Fraud Scoring as Reasoning Framework** — NOT numerical thresholds:
   - Explain that the Fraud Analyst evaluates claim against known patterns
   - Score reflects confidence that fraud patterns are present, not a binary yes/no
   - Higher scores = more patterns present + stronger indicators
   - The score informs the Senior Reviewer's decision — it does not auto-deny
   - Framework: "Consider the totality of indicators. A single red flag warrants noting. Multiple converging indicators warrant escalation. Pattern match to known fraud types warrants SIU referral."

4. **SIU Referral Criteria** — When to recommend Special Investigations Unit:
   - Hard fraud pattern match with supporting evidence
   - Organized ring indicators (multiple related claims)
   - Claim value above threshold with multiple red flags
   - Claimant has prior fraud history
   - SIU referral is a recommendation, not a denial — the claim may still be partially valid

5. **Fraud Statistics for Q&A** — Numbers the team should know:
   - $308.6 billion annual fraud cost to U.S. economy
   - Staged accidents up 47% in Q4 2024 (Aviva Canada data)
   - Approximately 10% of property-casualty claims involve some element of fraud
   - SIU referral rate: typically 5-8% of all claims

6. **Anti-Pattern Warning** — What NOT to do:
   - Do NOT auto-deny based on fraud score alone
   - Do NOT use hardcoded numerical thresholds ("if score > 70 then deny")
   - Fraud Analyst flags and recommends; Senior Reviewer decides
   - Finance never pays without Senior Reviewer approval
   - This separation is both correct insurance practice and prevents bad faith exposure

Format as a pattern catalog with clear descriptions suitable for AGENTS.md embedding. Include a quick-reference table of all patterns.
