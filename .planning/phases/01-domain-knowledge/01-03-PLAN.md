  <action>
Create TWO reference documents:

**Document 1: regulatory-compliance.md**

1. **Fair Claims Settlement Practices (FCSP) Act Timelines** — The numbers the team must know:
   - Claim acknowledgment: 10-15 business days from FNOL receipt (NAIC model: 10 days; California: 15 calendar days; use most conservative applicable standard)
   - Coverage decision: 40 calendar days after proof of loss received
   - Payment after acceptance: 30 calendar days
   - Frame as: "These are regulatory windows. The system must track elapsed time from FNOL and flag when approaching deadlines."
   - Note: Ohio follows NAIC model act — frame Q&A answers around NAIC standards

2. **Documentation Retention Requirements**:
   - Minimum 5 years for claim files (varies by state)
   - All agent decisions must be documented with reasoning (audit log)
   - Every status change timestamped
   - Why this matters: regulators can audit claim files years later

3. **Bad Faith Exposure Triggers** — When the insurer faces liability:
   - Unreasonable delay in acknowledging claim
   - Failure to investigate promptly and thoroughly
   - Denying claim without reasonable basis
   - Failing to communicate claim status to policyholder
   - Low-balling: offering substantially less than claim is worth
   - Misrepresenting policy provisions to deny coverage
   - Failing to provide written explanation for denial
   - How the system prevents bad faith: documented reasoning on every decision, timeline tracking, escalation triggers when approaching deadlines

4. **Regulatory Compliance as Reasoning Framework**:
   - Not "set timer to 15 days" but "acknowledge within the regulatory window for the relevant jurisdiction; default to the most conservative applicable standard"
   - The system should track compliance automatically and flag risks
   - Senior Reviewer should see timeline status before making decisions

**Document 2: payment-subrogation.md**

1. **Payment Calculation Methodology**:
   - Standard payout: damage estimate minus deductible
   - ACV policy: damage estimate minus deductible minus depreciation
   - RCV (Replacement Cost Value) policy: full replacement cost minus deductible (less common in auto)
   - Total loss payout: ACV minus deductible minus any salvage retention credit
   - Explain ACV vs RCV clearly — ACV accounts for depreciation, RCV does not

2. **Subrogation Logic** — When and how the insurer recovers:
   - Definition: insurer pays insured, then "steps into insured's shoes" to recover from at-fault third party
   - Trigger: at-fault party is a third party with identifiable insurance
   - Subrogation lien: insurer's right to recovery placed on the claim
   - Process: pay insured → identify at-fault insurer → file demand → negotiate/recover
   - Impact on claim closure: claim remains open for subrogation recovery even after payment
   - Deductible recovery: if subrogation successful, insured gets deductible back

3. **Rental Reimbursement Rules**:
   - Coverage must be on the policy (not all policies include it)
   - Standard allowance: $30-50/day, typically 30-day cap
   - Repair scenario: rental days = estimated repair days
   - Total loss scenario: typically 10 days max (time to locate replacement vehicle)
   - Rental class: comparable vehicle, not upgrade

4. **Depreciation and ACV Calculation Principles**:
   - ACV = replacement cost minus depreciation
   - Depreciation factors: age, mileage, condition, local market comparables
   - Not a formula — professional judgment based on market data
   - Common data sources: KBB, NADA, local dealer pricing
   - Explain the principle, not a specific calculation method

Format both as reasoning frameworks with Quick Reference sections.

  <action>
Create a comprehensive edge case catalog covering every scenario judges are likely to raise during Q&A. This is the team's "cheat sheet" for handling tough questions.

**Edge Case Categories:**

1. **Total Loss Handling**:
   - Ohio 100% ACV rule — when repair cost equals or exceeds ACV
   - Salvage title and salvage value
   - Policyholder retains salvage option (reduced payout)
   - GAP insurance awareness: when loan balance exceeds ACV, GAP covers the difference (mention awareness, don't build)
   - Total loss + rental: typically 10-day rental cap after total loss declaration

2. **Pre-existing Damage**:
   - Detection indicators: rust on impact areas, oxidation, damage in non-impact zones, prior repair evidence
   - Impact on estimate: only incident-related damage is covered
   - Conditional approval pending physical inspection
   - Dispute resolution: independent appraisal process

3. **Diminished Value (17c Formula)**:
   - Definition: loss in resale value after a repaired collision, even with perfect repair
   - The 17c formula (State Farm's method, widely referenced):
     - Base DV = 10% of pre-accident retail value (KBB/NADA)
     - Damage modifier: 0.00 to 1.00 based on severity (severe structural = 1.0, minor outer = 0.25)
     - Mileage modifier: 0.00 to 1.00 based on odometer (0-19,999 = 1.0, 100,000+ = 0.00)
     - Formula: Base DV × Damage Modifier × Mileage Modifier = Diminished Value
   - Note: Many states require insurers to address DV claims
   - For hackathon: Senior Reviewer should note DV awareness when applicable, but don't build the calculator
   - Know the formula for Q&A defense

4. **Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)**:
   - UM: at-fault party has NO insurance → claim against insured's own UM coverage
   - UIM: at-fault party has INSUFFICIENT insurance → claim against insured's UIM coverage for the gap
   - Different from collision: UM/UIM is a separate coverage with its own limits and deductibles
   - Routing: Claims Officer identifies UM/UIM scenario early → different coverage path
   - For hackathon: know the concept, mention in Q&A, show the field exists in schema

5. **Claim Reopening / Supplements**:
   - Hidden damage discovered during repair (behind panels, structural)
   - Supplement request: Assessor flags "hidden damage likely" → Senior Reviewer pre-authorizes supplement threshold
   - Supplement payment: Finance has supplement payment path (additional payment on same claim)
   - Not a new claim — same claim ID, additional estimate, additional payment
   - Common: 30-40% of collision claims have supplements

6. **Weather Surge / CAT (Catastrophic) Events**:
   - Definition: natural disaster causing large volume of claims in concentrated area/time
   - Impact on processing: surge capacity, prioritization, potential for temporary relaxed timelines
   - Coverage type: weather damage is comprehensive (not collision)
   - CAT tag affects: processing priority, adjuster assignment, potential for expedited inspection
   - Fraud risk in CAT events: prior damage claimed as storm damage, inflated estimates
   - For hackathon: Front Desk tags CAT events, Assessor and Fraud Analyst aware of CAT context

7. **Claim Denial Bad Faith Risk**:
   - Every denial must be documented with specific policy provision cited
   - Policyholder must receive written explanation
   - Denial must be based on reasonable investigation
   - "We deny because we think it's fraud" without investigation = bad faith
   - Proper denial path: investigate → document findings → cite exclusion or policy provision → written denial → appeal rights

8. **Legal Representation**:
   - When insured hires attorney → claim dynamics change
   - All communication goes through attorney
   - Increases claim value expectation
   - Escalation trigger for Senior Reviewer (human-in-the-loop)
   - Not adversarial per se — insured has right to representation

9. **Multi-Vehicle Incidents**:
   - Multiple claimants, multiple policies, complex liability
   - Each vehicle = separate claim, but linked for investigation
   - Fraud risk: staged multi-vehicle incidents (swoop-and-squat)
   - Subrogation complexity: multiple at-fault parties

10. **Policy Endorsements / Riders**:
    - Non-standard additions to base policy
    - Examples: rental car endorsement, roadside assistance, OEM parts endorsement, gap coverage
    - Must check endorsements during coverage verification
    - Endorsement can override standard policy terms

Format as a Q&A-ready catalog. For each edge case: what it is, why judges might ask about it, what the system does (or acknowledges), and a one-sentence answer the team can give.
